Bots now **read your documents**: PDFs (scanned ones too), images, Word, Excel, PowerPoint and OpenDocument files,
e-mails and archives, right in their sandbox, with nothing to install. They also **keep working on long tasks by
themselves**: no more "shall I continue?" after every answer.

## Bots read any document in their sandbox

Every bot's sandbox now ships with document tools (ADR-0043). Ask a bot to read a file or a whole folder, and it reads
it there:

- **PDFs**, including scans. Pages without text are read with OCR, in Spanish and English.
- **Images** (PNG, JPEG, GIF, WEBP, TIFF, BMP, AVIF, HEIC): text in a photo or a screenshot.
- **Office and OpenDocument files**: Word, Excel and PowerPoint, old formats too, plus their OpenDocument versions.
  Spreadsheets come out sheet by sheet.
- **Text formats** (CSV, JSON, XML and HTML), **e-mails** (`.eml`, Outlook `.msg`) and **archives** (zip, 7z, tar),
  whose contents are listed.
- **A whole folder or archive**, read in the background in chunks. A pack of thousands of files is read until every
  file is done, and the originals are never changed.
- **Nothing to install.** Bots no longer try `pip install` or `apt-get` for this and no longer report "missing tools".
  A file attached in a thread goes into the sandbox (`ocw_sandbox_import`) and is read there.
- **Existing bots get it by themselves.** A bot's sandbox moves to the new image at its next start, keeping its files
  and packages.
- **A larger sandbox image**: about 1.1 GB on disk instead of about 0.4 GB, and about 270 MB more to download.

**After upgrading:** a sandbox left alone picks up the new image after its 15-minute idle stop. **Restart** on a bot's
**Sandbox** tab moves a running sandbox at once, and stops what it was running. `docker image prune` then removes the
old image. Details: [docs/self-hosting/sandbox.md › Documents in the sandbox](https://github.com/VictorMinemu/open-collaborative-ai-workspace/blob/main/docs/self-hosting/sandbox.md#documents-in-the-sandbox).

## Bots keep working on long tasks

A bot no longer stops after each answer, and no longer asks "shall I continue?" (ADR-0042). When a request needs
more than one turn, it starts a **task** and keeps going in the thread by itself until the work is done:

- **It keeps going.** The bot works one step, notes its progress and its next step, and gets its next turn in the same
  thread a few seconds later. Nobody has to write "continue". It says when it is done, and when it needs you it says
  so and mentions you.
- **It waits instead of asking.** A long command in its sandbox, such as reading a whole folder with
  `ocw-extract --batch`, runs in the background and wakes the bot when it ends. So does each turn of a Work Zone
  session the bot started. A bot can also wait for a set time, up to an hour.
- **The task card.** The thread shows one live card: the goal, its state (Working, Waiting, Done, Blocked, Stopped),
  "Turn 7 of 40", the elapsed time, what it is waiting for, its last progress and its next step. It updates in place
  every turn.
- **Quiet while it works.** The thread gets a progress message at most every 5 turns or 10 minutes, and one at the
  end. Writing in the thread meanwhile never pauses or ends the task: the bot answers you as usual.
- **Stop.** **Stop** on the card ends the task at once: no new turn starts, and a turn already running finishes. It
  is shown to the person the task runs for, the channel's moderators and the people who manage the bot.
- **Limits you set.** Settings › Bot policy › **Long tasks** (on by default, for team and personal bots), and below it
  **Turns per task** (40 by default, up to 200), **Hours per task** (8, up to 24) and **Tasks running at once** in the
  organization (10, up to 100; 0 turns long tasks off). A task keeps the limits it started with. Each bot also has
  its own **Long tasks** switch in Bots › the bot › Capabilities.
- **When a limit ends it,** the task stops and the bot says so in the thread, mentioning the person it runs for: "I
  stopped this task after 40 turns, the limit for one task. The card shows where it got to."
- **The person who asked pays.** Every turn of a task runs for the person who started it, with their access decided
  again each turn, and counts in the bot's daily limit. A task never lets a turn do more than a normal one: after
  outside content it stays tainted, and its turns have no browser. It stops when that person can no longer read the
  conversation, or when the bot is turned off or removed.
- **Recorded.** The audit log shows each start, continuation, finish and stop with ids and counts, never the goal or
  the bot's notes.
- **Where.** Channels, threads and DMs with a bot. Not in AI chats yet.

## Fixes

- **Work Zone wake-ups reach the bot again after the first one.** OpenClaw dropped every server-originated turn on the
  same card after the first as a duplicate: the Work Zone wake-ups of a session since v0.3.0, and the continuations
  of a task. The plugin now gives each delivery, and each retry of it, its own inbound id.
- **Installing a marketplace plugin** could fail when OpenClaw was busy reloading its configuration. The install is
  now retried.
- **One-click runtime updates** answered "busy" right after a failed or timed-out update. The next one now waits for
  the earlier run to let go, then goes on.

## Upgrade

In `/opt/ocw` (or wherever the server is installed):

```bash
sudo node scripts/ocw.mjs upgrade --check   # current and newest release; changes nothing
sudo node scripts/ocw.mjs upgrade           # backup, this release, new keys, rebuild; never goes backwards
```

- **One new migration, additive** (long bot tasks: two tables and a few new columns with defaults). Existing bots,
  threads, cards, automations and Work Zone wake-ups keep working as before.
- **Two new job queues,** `bot_task.continue` and `bot_task.tick`, created by the `migrate` service with the rest of
  the database. Nothing new to run: the API's job workers run them
  ([docs/self-hosting/automations.md](https://github.com/VictorMinemu/open-collaborative-ai-workspace/blob/main/docs/self-hosting/automations.md#what-runs-where)).
- **Bot sandboxes** move to the new image by themselves (see *After upgrading* above).
- **Headless Work Zone servers** on v0.3.1 update their runtime with one click (Settings › Devices › the server ›
  Runtime updates › **Update**), or by themselves at the *Automatic* level. A server still on v0.3.0 needs the install
  command once, as the v0.3.1 notes say.

The desktop app does not update itself: install the new files below. Its runtime ships inside it.

## Install the server

On Ubuntu 24.04 or Debian 12, with a DNS name pointing at the server:

```bash
curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/VictorMinemu/open-collaborative-ai-workspace/main/scripts/install.sh \
  | sudo bash -s -- --domain ocw.your-company.com
```

The installer installs this release (`v0.3.2`). The [README tutorial](https://github.com/VictorMinemu/open-collaborative-ai-workspace#install-it-on-a-server)
walks through the first admin, model providers, invites and what to try first.

## Desktop app

None of the builds is signed yet, so the first launch needs one extra step:

- **macOS** (Apple silicon): open `OCW-v0.3.2-macos-arm64.dmg` and drag OCW to Applications. Open it once, then go to
  **System Settings › Privacy & Security** and choose **Open Anyway** (or run
  `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/OCW.app`).
- **Windows** (x64): run `OCW-v0.3.2-windows-x64-setup.exe`. If SmartScreen stops it, choose **More info › Run
  anyway**. It installs for the current user, without admin rights.
- **Linux** (x64): on Ubuntu or Debian, `sudo apt install ./OCW-v0.3.2-linux-amd64.deb`. Elsewhere,
  `chmod +x OCW-v0.3.2-linux-x86_64.AppImage` and run it. The app keeps the device token in the desktop keyring
  (GNOME Keyring or KWallet), which must be available.

Then enter your workspace URL and pair the computer under **Settings › Devices › Add device › Desktop app**. On a
server without a screen, use the headless runtime instead (**Settings › Devices › Add a device › Server**). Signed
builds are planned for MVP 2.

## Known limitations

- Long tasks run in channels, threads and DMs, not in AI chats. A task stays in its own thread: it cannot span several
  conversations or bots, and there is no list of a bot's tasks yet (the card in the thread is the place to look).
- A very large job may need more than the 40 turns of the default. The card then says the task used its turns; an
  admin raises **Turns per task** and the person asks again.
- After **Stop**, a turn already running finishes before the task ends (OpenClaw cannot abort a turn).
- Bots read documents in Spanish and English OCR only, and do not transcribe audio or video.
- The desktop app does not update itself, and its builds are not signed yet.

The full list is in the [README](https://github.com/VictorMinemu/open-collaborative-ai-workspace#known-limitations).
Report problems as [issues](https://github.com/VictorMinemu/open-collaborative-ai-workspace/issues), and security
problems privately ([SECURITY.md](https://github.com/VictorMinemu/open-collaborative-ai-workspace/blob/main/SECURITY.md)).
