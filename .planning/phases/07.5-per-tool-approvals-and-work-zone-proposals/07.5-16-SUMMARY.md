# Phase 07.5 Plan 16: approvals under concurrency, replay, time, leaks and tenants Summary

Through the real agent-action routes, one approval is exactly one execution, whatever the timing, the retries or who
replays it. Its arguments stay in the stored action, with the people who decide or asked, and in the requester's own
DM card. The plan's own cases found no defect. Mutations of the product code show that each group of cases fails
when its guarantee is removed.

The coordinator's item from 07.5-13, a `deadlock detected` on a `user:` stream, reproduced in the first round. It is
fixed: approval transactions now take stream locks in the same order as a message post.

## Defects found and fixed

The plan's cases found none, and `tool-calls.ts` and `claim.ts` are unchanged. The coordinator's item found one.

### The 07.5-13 deadlock: approval stream fan-out took locks in the wrong order

**Root cause.** Every `realtime.appendEvent` upserts its stream's `app.streams` row, and that row lock is held until
commit. Two transactions that append to the same streams in different orders deadlock, and PostgreSQL aborts one with
a 500. The approval service broke the order a message post follows (the conversation, then `user:` streams, then the
cell) in two ways:

- `announce` went to `user:` streams in audience order, which puts the requester first. Sam's request was announced
  to [Sam, Marta] and Marta's to [Marta, Sam]. Their decisions were announced in crossed orders too.
- `request` announced to `user:` streams before posting the card on the conversation stream, and `decide` announced
  before mirroring the card. A message post in that conversation that notifies the same people locks the
  conversation and then their `user:` streams.

**Failing tests first.** Four cases were added to `tool-approvals-race.int`, under "fan-out to the same `user:` streams
from concurrent transactions never deadlocks". Each runs 6 rounds:

| Case | At the same moment | Before the fix |
|---|---|---|
| Crossed requests | Sam's and Marta's bots ask (a `users` rule naming both, so each request is announced to both) | 500, `deadlock detected` in `announce` |
| Crossed decisions | Marta decides Sam's requests while Sam decides Marta's | 500, deadlock |
| Request against posts | Sam's bot asks in #ops while Víctor's posts in #ops mention Sam and Marta | 500, deadlock (`announce` against `notification.deliver` and `lockNextSeq`) |
| Mentions in two channels | Posts mention the same two people, in opposite orders, in two channels | Passed before and after; not reproduced (notification fan-out stayed consistent) |

The first three fail against the unfixed files (checked by restoring them, then putting the fix back). With the fix,
the four pass 3 runs out of 3.

**Fix** (`506fe9e8`): one stream lock order, the one a message post already follows. The conversation comes first,
then `user:` streams in ascending id order, then the cell.

- `approval/service.ts`:
  - `announce` appends in ascending id order (`announcedTo`).
  - `request` first locks the card's conversation stream and its `user:` streams (new `realtime.lockStreams`), then
    posts the card, then announces. The lock comes first because the card's own post notifies the requester before
    the request is announced.
  - `decide` mirrors the card, announces, then emits the bot's card action. This uses the new
    `mirrorCardDecision({ beforeCardAction })` hook, which also runs when the card was already decided.
  - `consumeGrant` and `closeGone` mirror the card before announcing.
- `realtime/service.ts`: `lockStreams(tx, topics)` locks streams in the order given, creating missing ones at seq 0
  (as chat's `lockNextSeq` does).
- `chat/card-actions.ts`: the `beforeCardAction` hook.

**Blast radius.** The realtime events are the same: the same types, payloads and streams, and every stream's seq is
still gapless. Only their order inside one transaction changed, and clients receive them only after commit. No
permission, audit or approval rule changed. Every approval, chat, memory, tool-approval, agent-channel, work-session,
realtime and notification integration test passes unchanged.

**Not changed (candidates below).**

- Transaction retry on `40P01` was not added. It would touch `withTenant` for every module.
- A `server_apply` approval's own writes (`memory.changed` fan-out) still run before the card is mirrored.
- Notification fan-out keeps recipient order.

## WINDOWS #219

The contention half of #219 is closed by its test: "approval cards posted inside the claim transaction under
concurrent channel traffic". At once in #ops:

- 24 human messages from Sam and Marta, 4 of them mentioning Keeper (new turns);
- 4 approval calls (3 actions, one of them twice from another turn);
- 2 denials of earlier requests (card mirrors and card-action turns).

Every request answers 200, in under 15 s. There is no deadlock and no 500. The calls make 3 approvals and 3 cards,
all in #ops. Both denials hold. The `conv:<ops>` stream has every seq after the burst exactly once, with no gap, and
each new message has its event.

The other two parts of #219 are outside this plan and stay open:

- the logical connector name in a real cell (07.5-15 smoke);
- the fake MCP has no 5xx after a consumed grant.

The ledger itself was not edited, as instructed.

### Added cases (stronger, same files)

- **Two turns.** The same call from two turns of Sam at once still gives one approval.
- **Re-run turn against Sam's next turn.** Both race for one grant: one execution, three `approval_used`. The hash
  binds agent, requester, conversation, tool and arguments, not the event (P-05).
- **The plugin's retry at once** (same toolCallId, concurrently): one approval.
- **Scribe naming Keeper's re-run turn**: `unknown_turn`, nothing consumed.
- **The other side of the window.** A grant at 14 minutes is still used.
- **Keychain.** Two concurrent re-runs of one approved `POST` make one outbound request.
- **The reverse tenant direction.** Org A's token with org B's turn gives `unknown_turn`. Org B's people cannot open
  or decide org A's approval: 404, and it stays pending.

### Adapted

1. **Another bot in the same DM.** A 1:1 DM holds one bot, so the grant is made in a group DM: Sam (its single human),
   Keeper and Scribe. Scribe's turn there, for Sam, with the same tool and arguments, opens a new approval.
2. **The ruled DM sweep uses a `users` rule naming Marta**, not the requester rule. The decider (Marta) and the
   requester (Sam) are then two people, and both views are checked. Dana and the admin Víctor get 404.
3. **The 07.4-12 walker is copied into `tool-args-leak.int.test.ts`, not imported.** It is not exported, and
   `connectors/security.int.test.ts` is outside this plan's files. The copy adds row-level locations: which stream,
   event type and card message a hit belongs to.
4. **Cross-tenant on the proxy routes is 403 `no_trigger`**, as the plan allows ("404 or denied decision"). On
   `/tool-calls` it is 200 `unknown_turn`. In both cases no approval or ledger row is written in org A.
5. **Time travel** moves `created_at`, `expires_at` and `decided_at` together through the superuser client, so the
   order of the three columns holds.
6. **TDD commits.** These are `test(api)` commits only: there is no product change, so there is no `feat` gate. RED is
   shown by the mutations above.
7. **Branch.** The commits are on `wt/07.5-16` as instructed. That name is outside the executor's `agent-*` allow-list,
